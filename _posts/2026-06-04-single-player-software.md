Every morning at 6 a.m. my Mac mini prints out my day.

<!--more-->

A small piece of software I wrote (with Claude) reaches into my Google Calendar and my to-do list, pulls everything I'm supposed to do or be at, lays it out on half a sheet of letter paper, and saves it as a PDF. The PDF goes to the printer. The printout goes into my notebook. By the time I sit down at the desk it's waiting for me — paper, no screen, things to cross off in pen.

At the end of the day I plan tomorrow. I cross things off in the digital tools, schedule whatever moved, and the next morning the cycle starts again.

I love this setup. I love the tactile satisfaction of crossing things off. I love being able to work without a glowing rectangle in front of me. And I love that I don't have to give up the things digital is genuinely better at — recurring tasks, "first Monday of every month," meetings other humans can see and join.

But the real reason I'm writing about it isn't the productivity hack. It's that **this little piece of software shouldn't exist.**

A piece of automation this specific to one person's quirks — print-and-paste, half-letter, *my* tools, *my* calendar, *my* layout — would never have been worth a software engineer's time. Nobody hires someone to build them this. I wouldn't have built this two years ago, or even six months ago, and I can (sort of) write code. There were too many other things to do.

But something changed. The cost of making a small, weird, personal piece of software collapsed. With Claude, "I want a thing that does X for me" went from a few days (maybe) to a few minutes.

This is going to keep happening. And I think we're about to live in a world full of small, custom, slightly weird software that exists only for the person who wanted it. Single-player software.

Of course, none of this is entirely new. People have been writing little automations for themselves for decades. If you read what I just described and thought "okay, you wrote a cron job," you're not wrong. So the *idea* isn't new. What's new is who gets to do it. Until very recently, "I want a thing that prints my day on half-letter paper at 6 a.m." was either a weekend project for someone fluent in a stack of tools, or it wasn't going to exist. Now it's 10 minutes for anyone with a Claude Code or Codex subscription. The set of people who can build something like this just got much bigger. The skill that matters isn't syntax — it's taste, knowing what's worth automating in the first place.

Tomorrow at 6 a.m. my printout will be waiting for me — small and weird and exactly mine. What will you build?
